01 / 04PROPERTY & CONVEYANCING

Buying land in Kenya: begin with verification

Before committing funds to a property transaction, take time to establish what interest is being sold, who has authority to sell it and whether the title or register discloses restrictions. A sale agreement should reflect the transaction accurately.

  • Obtain relevant registry information and conduct appropriate searches.
  • Review the identity and authority of the seller and any required consents.
  • Examine the agreement, completion documents and intended mode of payment.
  • Confirm the correct transfer and registration procedure for the property.

Due diligence is transaction-specific. Information available from a search may not address every potential land-related concern.

02 / 04CORPORATE & COMMERCIAL

Commercial contracts: clauses worth careful attention

A contract should do more than record the headline price. Clear drafting can reduce uncertainty about who must do what, when performance is due and what happens when circumstances change.

  • Describe the scope of services, goods or obligations with care.
  • Review payment terms, milestones, warranties and delivery dates.
  • Understand termination rights and consequences of breach.
  • Consider dispute resolution, governing law and confidentiality.

The enforceability and suitability of any clause depends on the agreement and applicable law.

03 / 04FAMILY & SUCCESSION

Succession and probate: understanding the first steps

The administration of a deceased person’s estate can involve identifying assets and liabilities, locating relevant testamentary documents, determining the persons entitled to apply and obtaining an appropriate grant.

  • Determine whether there is a valid will and identify estate documents.
  • Compile information about assets, liabilities and relevant family relationships.
  • Consider the proper application for probate or letters of administration.
  • Understand the distinction between a grant, confirmation and distribution.

Individual circumstances and the Law of Succession Act may alter the route and documentation required.

04 / 04LANDLORD & TENANT

Commercial tenancies and the Business Premises Rent Tribunal

Commercial premises do not all fall within the same statutory framework. Certain tenancies of shops, hotels and catering establishments may qualify as controlled tenancies and be subject to special notice and tribunal procedures.

  • Examine whether the premises and tenancy fall within the relevant legislation.
  • Check the lease terms, duration and termination provisions.
  • Avoid assuming that an ordinary demand or eviction procedure always applies.
  • Seek advice on notices, rent claims and tribunal jurisdiction.

Timelines and remedies differ by legal process; verify the correct procedure before taking action.
